Core investor deck

Facilitator-led protected dialogue: private written rooms, then an approved record with a verification anchor — Configure → Verify → Facilitate → Release. Room and record stay separate by design.

July 2026 · Assumption-labeled · Private pilot stage

What the company is

Purpose-built for high-stakes written dialogue — not generic chat.

Problem

Sensitive coordination fails when identity is fully public and unsafe, or fully unverified and not credible — and when there is no integrity-checked outcome.

Solution

SquadRidge runs facilitator-led private rooms, then releases only approved outcomes with a SHA-256 verification anchor — optional public ledger or private memo.

Mission

Give mediators and institutions a calm process: protected dialogue, then a citable record — without publishing who said what.

Market gap

The failure mode is not messaging volume. It is trust structure.

Why existing modes break
  • Public-identity rooms can deter candid participation when stakes are political, institutional, or safety-sensitive.
  • Anonymous channels increase candor but often weaken credibility, moderation leverage, and downstream usability.
  • Unstructured exchanges create drift, escalation, and poor traceability for decision-makers who need briefing-grade outputs.
Who feels the pain
  • Facilitators running high-friction conversations where trust is fragile and safety constraints matter.
  • Pilot sponsors that need process discipline, boundaries, and evidence of what was actually said or proposed.
  • Organizations exploring conflict-prevention, dialogue, and de-escalation workflows without overexposing participants.

Product spine

Configure → Verify → Facilitate → Release — room and record stay separate.

1 · Configure

Invite-only sessions with role-scoped access; NGO deliberation is the default pilot template.

2 · Verify

Facilitator verifies eligibility privately; identities are confirmed but not disclosed on the public record.

3 · Facilitate

Structured written rounds in a private room — nothing said in the room is published as a transcript.

4 · Release

Approved outcome text only, with hash-bound approvals and a verification anchor partners can recompute.

Why now

Institutional demand is rising for safer coordination with explicit security boundaries.

Trust pressure

Teams increasingly need mechanisms that are more credible than open anonymity and safer than full public identity.

Operational pressure

Sponsors want facilitated rooms and process evidence, not just transcripts or chat logs that create more ambiguity.

Governance pressure

The current release already frames scope honestly, which is increasingly important in security-sensitive software procurement.

Early use model

The initial go-to-market is high-touch and pilot-led.

Near-term motion
  • Start with facilitator-led pilots where trust boundaries and structured operations are central to success.
  • Sell implementation discipline, room format, and output quality before broad self-serve product expansion.
  • Use the demo, security disclosure, and sample ledger artifacts to support diligence and sponsor conversations.
