Value chain

Who pays, who runs the room

Economic buyer

Program sponsor paying for a bounded cohort, review pack, and reporting motion—not a self-serve seat land grab.

Operational owner

Facilitator or program manager who owns cohort quality, training, and the partner readout.

Pilot packaging

Cohort-sized, not self-serve PLG

Early packages should look like 6–12 week cohorts: setup, facilitator alignment, live sessions, and a written readout. This is intentionally high-touch before any broader expansion motion.

Unit framing

Seat / room-month (illustrative)

The model uses seat-month pricing as a clean planning unit, but real contracts can blend cohort minimums, named facilitator seats, reporting work, and review-pack add-ons.

Contract language should reflect how the partner counts active participants, observers, and staff—not just the internal model variable name.

Illustrative bridge

Not ARR — modeled pathway

The modeled bridge is: win a narrow pilot, earn a second cohort or longer pilot term, then expand into repeat program operations only after evidence and governance survive the first engagement.

Mix

Software vs facilitation services

Early revenue will likely mix software access with setup, training, facilitation support, and reporting. Keep that mix explicit so the deck does not overstate software gross margin at the current stage.

Expansion (target)

Land → deepen within orgs

  • Additional cohorts or geographies within the same sponsor once pilot governance works.
  • Paid reporting, review-pack, or security diligence support when buyers need it.
  • Eventually: recurring program infrastructure—but only after pilot evidence is real.
