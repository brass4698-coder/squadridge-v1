# Pilot Partner One-Pager

## What MENDguild Is

MENDguild is a pilot-stage dialogue platform for small, structured, cross-border cohorts. It is designed for organizations that need more verification, safety framing, and session structure than general-purpose chat or meeting tools provide.

**Strategic “missing link” (roadmap context):** Many actors need **trustworthy structure** for sensitive contact *before* situations harden—not only after violence. That prevention-minded framing guides long-term product direction; **today’s pilots** remain bounded verification, session, and accountability workflows—see [`strategic-positioning-early-warning.md`](strategic-positioning-early-warning.md) and [`../security/threat-model.md`](../security/threat-model.md) for honesty on what is shipped vs vision.

## Best-Fit Early Partner

The strongest early partner is an organization already convening sensitive dialogue, such as:

- peacebuilding NGOs
- research labs
- Track II and Track 1.5 programs
- university conflict-resolution initiatives

## What A Pilot Looks Like

A typical first pilot is:

- one bounded participant cohort
- one partner organization
- one facilitator or moderator lead
- one to three structured sessions
- pre-session onboarding and post-session feedback

## What The Product Contributes

- verification-oriented access flow
- small-group session structure
- clearer safety and trust posture than generic tools
- operator support for moderation and pilot operations
- documentation and reporting for partner learning

## Current Boundaries

MENDguild is a serious pilot foundation, not yet a finished institution-grade platform. Partners should understand:

- the product is best used in bounded, facilitator-supported pilots
- current privacy and operator-visibility boundaries are described in [`../security/threat-model.md`](../security/threat-model.md)
- messaging should not be described as Signal-style end-to-end encryption against the platform
- impact claims should stay grounded in the metrics actually collected during the pilot

## What The Partner Provides

- participant cohort definition
- facilitator or program owner
- pilot goals and success criteria
- participant support and communication channel
- post-session feedback participation

## What Success Looks Like

A strong early pilot shows:

- participants can onboard and verify successfully
- sessions start and complete reliably
- facilitator confidence improves versus generic tools
- incidents are low and handled well
- participants report the sessions were constructive and worth repeating
