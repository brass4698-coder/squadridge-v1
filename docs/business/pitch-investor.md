## Slide 2 — The Problem

**The tools we use to bridge conflict are making it worse.**

Today's peacebuilders — facilitators, mediators, NGO workers, diaspora activists — cobble together Zoom, Signal, and Slack to run cross-border dialogue. These tools were built for corporate productivity, not high-stakes conflict environments.

**Three critical failures:**

1. **Identity exposure kills participation.** In Ukraine, Gaza, and Sudan, revealing your name, location, or stated view can get you arrested — or killed. Zoom and Google Workspace offer zero meaningful anonymization.

2. **Social platforms amplify outrage.** Twitter/X and Facebook are optimized for engagement, not understanding. Affective polarization deepens; dialogue collapses.

3. **The warning-response gap.** Policymakers and UN agencies lack real-time, ground-level sentiment data from conflict zones. Early warnings arrive too late, or not at all.

**The result:** 1.8 billion people live in fragile or conflict-affected states (World Bank, 2024). The infrastructure for citizen peacebuilding simply does not exist.

## Slide 3 — The Solution

**SquadRidge: Verified-anonymous squads that build bridges across conflict lines.**

SquadRidge is a purpose-built, encrypted dialogue platform. We match 4–6 participants from opposing sides of a conflict into secure, time-bound sessions — with no PII ever exposed.

**How it works:**

- **Zero-Knowledge Verification** — Users prove relevant attributes (citizenship, organizational role) using Semaphore-based ZK proofs. The system knows you qualify. It never knows who you are.
- **Structured Small-Group Matching** — An algorithm pairs verified participants across conflict lines into intimate squads designed for sustained empathy, not viral broadcasting.
- **AI De-escalation Layer** — Real-time tone detection surfaces rising tension. A one-tap "Slow down" pause and a "Pull back" message-retraction feature give users the Power of Pause before escalation takes hold.
- **Early Warning Signal Layer** — Aggregated, de-identified sentiment metrics flow to vetted mediators, think tanks, and UN agencies — without ever compromising individual privacy.

Built on React + TypeScript, Supabase (PostgreSQL + Realtime), and Supabase Edge Functions (Deno). Privacy anchored by the Enclave[ZK] stack from Enclave Health Technologies Inc. (EHTI).

2. **ZK cryptography has crossed the usability threshold.** Browser-native ZK proofs are now feasible. Semaphore-based verification runs client-side without specialized hardware. The privacy-preserving stack that was a research paper in 2020 is shippable today.

3. **Peace-tech investment is surging.** The sector is projected to reach $1B by 2025 (PeaceTech Accelerator data). Specialized funders — Anadyr, impact-first VCs, the MacArthur and Open Society Foundations — are actively seeking infrastructure plays. No one has built the platform layer yet.

## Slide 6 — Business Model

**Three interlocking revenue streams:**

**1. Institutional SaaS Licensing (B2B / B2G)**
Tiered annual licenses for peacebuilding NGOs, UN agencies, and Track II diplomacy programs. Includes facilitator dashboards, session management, organizational analytics.
*Target ASP: $10K–$75K/yr per organization.*

**2. Early Warning Data Subscriptions (B2G)**
Aggregated, de-identified sentiment streams sold to vetted mediators, think tanks, and policy analysts.
*Target ASP: $25K–$100K/yr per subscriber.*

**3. Grant & Philanthropic Funding (Non-dilutive)**
USIP (~$70–100K), MacArthur Foundation, Open Society Foundations, AI for Peace / DeepFunding, SBIR/STTR (up to $1.25M phased). This capital is not "grant dependency" — it is the market norm for peace-tech infrastructure at early stage.

**Free tier for citizens.** The core matching and dialogue experience is always free for The Concerned Citizen and The Diaspora Member — mission-critical for adoption in low-income conflict zones.
