# User Personas

## Overview

MENDguild is designed to serve a diverse range of users across the peacebuilding ecosystem. Our user personas reflect the distinct needs and challenges of citizens, practitioners, and analysts engaging in cross-border dialogue [1].

## 1. The Concerned Citizen

**Profile**: A resident of a conflict-affected region or a diaspora member deeply invested in the outcome of a conflict.

**Goals**:
*   To connect safely with individuals from the "other side" to understand their perspectives.
*   To participate in dialogue without fear of state surveillance or retaliation [1].
*   To contribute to peacebuilding efforts at a grassroots level.

**Pain Points**:
*   Traditional social media amplifies outrage and polarization [2].
*   Lack of secure, anonymous platforms for cross-border communication [3].
*   Fear of personal information (PII) exposure in high-risk environments [1].

**How MENDguild Helps**:
*   Provides verified anonymity through zero-knowledge proofs, ensuring their identity remains hidden [1].
*   Matches them into small, structured squads for meaningful dialogue [1].
*   Offers AI-assisted translation and de-escalation tools to facilitate understanding [1].

## 2. The Peace Practitioner

**Profile**: A facilitator, mediator, or NGO worker involved in Track II or Track 1.5 diplomacy.

**Goals**:
*   To run structured, secure digital dialogue sessions across conflict lines [1].
*   To scale their peacebuilding efforts beyond physical, in-person workshops [3].
*   To monitor sentiment and identify emerging narratives within the dialogue [2].

**Pain Points**:
*   Existing tools like Zoom or Google Workspace lack meaningful anonymization and security [3].
*   Difficulty in managing "information overload" and tracking sentiment in large-scale dialogues [2].
*   Challenges in ensuring diverse participation, especially from low-bandwidth regions [3].

**How MENDguild Helps**:
*   Provides a purpose-built, secure platform for structured digital dialogue [3].
*   Offers facilitator tools and organizational dashboards to manage sessions [1].
*   Integrates AI analytics to surface hidden drivers of conflict and track sentiment [2].

## 3. The Policy Analyst

**Profile**: A researcher, intelligence analyst, or UN official monitoring geopolitical tensions.

**Goals**:
*   To detect rising regional tensions and early warning signals of conflict [1].
*   To understand the effectiveness of peacebuilding interventions [2].
*   To access aggregated, de-identified data for analysis without compromising user privacy [1].

**Pain Points**:
*   The "warning-response" gap: early warnings often fail to catalyze timely political action [3].
*   The "data winter": lack of localized, sensitive data for humanitarian AI applications [3].
*   Ethical concerns regarding the weaponization of data collected in conflict zones [3].

**How MENDguild Helps**:
*   Aggregates de-identified sentiment metrics to provide early warning insights [1].
*   Contributes to a "Data Commons" model, pooling diverse datasets for public benefit while maintaining participatory governance [2].
*   Ensures ethical safeguards and "do no harm" principles are embedded in the data architecture [2].
